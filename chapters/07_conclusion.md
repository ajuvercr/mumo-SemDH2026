# Conclusion and Future Work {#sec:conclusion}

This paper reported on MuMo (Museum Monitoring), a three-year applied research project that explored the use of dataspace principles for environmental monitoring in museum practice. By integrating semantic data publication, event-based access, and decentralized governance with existing monitoring infrastructure, MuMo addressed practical challenges related to long-running data collection, cross-institutional collaboration, and controlled data sharing.

A central outcome of the project is the demonstration that dataspace-oriented architectures can be deployed incrementally alongside legacy systems (RQ1). Concretely, the in-use scenarios (Section \ref{sec:scenarios}) show that semantic publication via LDES, combined with Solid-based governance, supports longitudinal analysis (Scenario 1), selective cross-institutional sharing during loans (Scenario 2), and client-side integration across independent deployments (Scenario 3)—addressing RQ2. The lessons learned (Section \ref{sec:lessons}) further illustrate that design choices grounded in conservatorial practice—such as group-based access control and client-side integration rather than server-side aggregation—can be both sufficient in practice and easier to sustain than more fine-grained or centralized alternatives (RQ3).

In addition, while the system supports aggregation across multiple MuMo deployments, integrating external data sources beyond the project has not yet been explored in deployed settings.

In conclusion, MuMo provides an in-use perspective on how Solid-based data governance, Linked Data Event Streams, and lightweight semantic modeling can support data management in cultural heritage institutions. While the monitored data is operational rather than humanistic in nature, the architectural patterns and lessons—decentralized publication, governance-aware access control, and semantic interoperability with legacy systems—are transferable to Digital Humanities projects that face similar challenges of long-running, distributed data under institutional governance.
